Terms used in this page (open once)
field-line style, axisymmetric style
The two vacuum methods, vac%style 1 and 2. Style 1 (VMEC) is the 3-D boundary-element method on the points of the field lines; style 2 (HELENA) integrates over ζ analytically with toroidal functions and over θ numerically.
BC_style
Boundary conditions; its second value is the plasma edge: 1 fixed, 2 free through the variational surface energy, 4 free through an explicit natural-boundary-condition row.
vac_diag_style
How the diagonals of the vacuum matrices G and H are computed: 1 indirect (from identities), 2 direct (corner term and cell integral). New input.
X, p
X = ξ·∇ψ, PB3D's normal displacement; p the parallel angle along a field line (θ for poloidal flux). A mode: X ∝ ei(nα + (nq−m)p).
EP, res
EP: the normal data of the vacuum potential for each mode at the boundary points (in calc_vac_res). res = vac%res, the nmod × nmod vacuum response that enters the edge block of A; res(m, m) its diagonal element for poloidal mode m.
lines × points
A field-line vacuum grid: number of field lines × points per line, e.g. 128 × 121 on W7-X.
the circular cases
HELENA circular-cross-section equilibria with q from 1.04 (fixtures circ_eps005_q104, circ_eps033_q104, inverse aspect ratio 0.05 and 0.33); the n = 10 external kink is the free-boundary benchmark.
VMEC twin, rippled twin
The VMEC equilibrium made from the ε = 0.33 HELENA case (same plasma, VMEC++), and copies of it with a 5-fold ripple of amplitude δ (0.01 to 0.04): the 3-D tests of the field-line style.
cbm18a, cut cbm18a
The HELENA pedestal equilibrium of the regression decks (n = 10, edge at ψ = 0.75 in the free-boundary deck), and a version of it cut at the pedestal foot for the comparison with TERPSICHORE.
MISHKA-1, TERPSICHORE
The axisymmetric code you used in your PhD (rebuilt from Simulations/MISHKA/Sources), and the open-source 3-D stability code of EPFL (since 2025), run on Snellius.
check_vac, CHECKVAC
New input that makes PB3D test its vacuum operators with exact potentials on the real boundary; the results are log lines starting with CHECKVAC.
anchor, fixture
An eigenvalue recorded in a regression test, and an equilibrium file committed for the tests (tests/fixtures).
eigenvalue
ω²/ωA² in PB3D's MISHKA normalisation (norm_style 1), negative when unstable.
Snellius
The SURF cluster; the runs of 27 October on were made there.

How to use this page

  • Each section has a status (to do, in progress, done, question) and a notes box. They are saved with the page, so they survive reloads and other devices, and Claude can read your notes back in a later session. When the HTML is opened as a local file they are kept in that browser only.
  • Every code change is in the page, in the section that explains it, and always as one diff from 2.47 to 9e9aba5: each section lists its commits as cards (subject, message, what the commit's own diff holds), then shows each file those commits changed once, with only the blocks of changed lines (hunks) they touched. The vacuum modules are shown routine by routine in stage 5. The intermediate states are not shown; each commit's own diff is one click away on GitHub. Under each block are the commits that changed its lines; the ten blocks that commits of two sections changed appear in both.
  • Blocks of the non-vacuum modules carry a badge from a mechanical classifier: use-list, ldebug-guard, moved and the like mean the block only moves or renames lines and was checked by script; read means it changes what the code does.
  • Line numbers are those of 2.47 (left) and of 9e9aba5 (right). A click on a number opens that line on GitHub at that version. In VS Code, Cmd+P and vac_ops.f90:1537 opens the line in your checkout.
  • Two switches in the index: "hide comment-only diff lines" (a quarter of the changed lines in vac_ops are comments) and "hide mechanical hunks", which leaves only what the classifier could not decide. Right above the code of each section, one button opens the files that have a block to read and jumps to the first of them; "close all files" closes them again. The index marks the section you are reading.
  • Numbers: a number without a named source comes from the commit message of the commit the section is about. Other sources are named. Numbers from the demos and tools are produced by this page. Where I derived something for this page, the text says so.
  • demo blocks are small scripts in review/demos/. Each prints numbers and draws one figure; output and figure are in the page, so running them is optional.
  • Tiers: skim the change is checked by a tool or is trivial; read read the diff with the explanation; study work through the reasoning before reading the code.
  • Two appendices. A, optional: the first eigenvalue of nine decks at every commit, to look up which commit moved which number. L, the ledger: every commit that touched a Fortran module and where its change appears here; the build script stops with an error if any change is missing.

V4

Field-line kernels and calc_GH_1

routines calc_GH, calc_GH_1, calc_GH_int_1time 1.5 hstudy

calc_GH. G and H are set to zero before both styles accumulate into them. After a Richardson step the field-line arrays still held the interlaced copy of the previous level, so 2.47 counted those contributions twice. Since your decision V0-a (937d6a8), calc_GH also sets the diagonals of the boundary rows for both styles, after the assembly of the style; V6 explains that block.

calc_GH_int_1, the kernels on one interval of a field line, is rewritten:

  • Far from the influence point: the kernel values at the two ends (the trapezoidal rule along the line), with H = n·(xs−x)/r³. 2.47 had (-G)**(-3) = r³ instead of 1/r³.
  • At the singular point: the exact integral of 1/r over the half cell in the metric of the surface (the closed form F(U, V, s) in the comment and in the demo). The 2.47 expression had the same logarithm twice and diverged like 1/s² for perpendicular coordinates. H gets 0 there; calc_GH sets the diagonals afterwards (V6).
  • Near the influence point (within n_near = 4 interval lengths, a parameter): 8-point Gauss–Legendre on each half interval, on the cubic interpolant through a four-point stencil. This is the fix of 31 October for W7-X: at the inboard bean tips neighbouring lines are 0.007 R₀ apart and the points along them 0.06 R₀, so the kernel along the neighbouring line is a peak much narrower than the point spacing. The point rule then gives order-one errors whose sign depends on how the points of neighbouring lines line up; the diagonal of vac%res on W7-X ranged from 44 to 126 depending on the grid (7 000 to 62 000 boundary points), sorted by the fractional part of q Δθ/Δα. With the near-field rule the five grids from 64 × 121 to 144 × 121 and 128 × 141 agree to 1.2 % on the whole diagonal, and the circular twin's free eigenvalue went from −0.5189 to −0.4491 against HELENA's −0.4438. The demo shows the same effect on a straight line.
  • The interval that bridges two field lines is skipped before any evaluation (2.47 evaluated it and dropped it with on_field_line).

calc_GH_1: the contributions get the weight Δp Δα / 2 per half interval, so G and H are true surface operators (consistent with the absolute −4π of the row sum); the α step is 2π/nα (vac_steps; 2.47 used 2π/(nα−1), 50c9511); at least 2 lines and 4 points per line are required. Since V0-a it only assembles the rows: the diagonal block at its end and its contained set_diag are gone, and the diagonals are in V6.

Then read the near branch: stencil parameter s, Lagrange weights l, the factor 0.5 for the half interval.
  3. Tests: GH_int_1_singular (brute force), GH_int_1_near (tolerance 3e-3, the error of the cubic interpolant), GH_int_1_regular, greens_identity_3d, point_source_3d, response_1_vs_2 (field-line style on an axisymmetric torus against the axisymmetric style: ratios 1.006 to 0.996, recorded).

V6

Diagonals: indirect and direct

routines the diagonal block of calc_GH, diag_indirect, set_pot_2, set_diag_vac; fit_diag, vac_steps, solid_angle_node, H_sing_node, calc_diag_direct, dis_diagtime 1 hstudy

Input vac_diag_style: 1 indirect (default), 2 direct.

  • Field-line, indirect: Hii from the row sum (as 2.47); Gii = the least-squares solution, one per row, of H x − G nx = −4π x for x, y, z. Until V0-a the code wrote it as the analytical half-cell value plus a correction in which the half-cell value cancelled (V0-a); since then it computes the fit in one step. The identities (31 October) put the error of the one-point rule across neighbouring lines into the diagonal.
  • Field-line, direct: Hii = −2π + the integral of the H kernel over the cell (calc_diag_direct, with H_sing_node = the corrected eq. 69 of the manuscript, erratum E22), the row sum at the line ends; Gii from the half cells alone. solid_angle_node computes the faceted solid angle (eq. 45); on the field-aligned mesh it is 16 % of 4π away from 2π (mesh directions 14° apart, erratum E6), so it is printed by check_vac only.
  • Axisymmetric, indirect: per row, least squares for (Hii, Gii) over the identities of Rⁿ, RⁿZ (c = −4π) and the ring-source potential at the centroid (c = 0). It made the axisymmetric style converge like 1/N³ in the poloidal points instead of 1/N: exact harmonics k = 6/10/15 at 601 points 4.6e-5/2.2e-4/7.4e-4 against 2.1e-3/4.6e-3/7.7e-3 with the direct diagonals. The convergence like 1/N of style 2 in HELENA's poloidal points, found on 1 November, came from this diagonal.

How the code does it since V0-a (937d6a8, pull request #31). One routine does the fit for both styles, and one routine chooses the potentials:

  • fit_diag in vac_utilities solves, for each boundary row i and in the least-squares sense over a list of potentials k with known boundary values φ and normal data φ′, the identity \[ h\,\phi_{k,i} - g\,\phi'_{k,i} = c_k\phi_{k,i} - \sum_{j\ne i} H_{ij}\phi_{k,j} + \sum_{j\ne i} G_{ij}\phi'_{k,j} \] for h = Hii, for g = Gii, or for both. It takes the off-diagonal sums as the products with the full matrices minus their current diagonals, so the matrices are not changed. An entry that is not fitted is passed in as known, and its term moves to the right-hand side. For both entries it solves the 2 × 2 normal equations by Cramer's rule, as diag_indirect_2 did; for one entry it divides once.
  • diag_indirect in vac_ops passes each style's potentials. For the field-line style it calls fit_diag twice: first for Hii from the constant, which gives the row sum, then for Gii from x, y and z with that Hii. For the axisymmetric style it calls it once, for both entries from the three potentials of set_pot_2, each scaled to unit maximum.
  • calc_GH, after the assembly, sets the indirect diagonals with vac_diag_style 1. With vac_diag_style 2 the axisymmetric style keeps the diagonals of its assembly (corner term and analytical integrals), and the field-line style gets the direct Hii where a node has a full cell, the row sum at the ends of the lines, and keeps the half-cell Gii.

calc_vac_res: data, projection, factor

routine calc_vac_res (set_up_data, solve_potential, project_response, finish_response)time 1 hstudy

The routine is split into four named internal steps that share the host's variables (225aa72). That commit says no statement changed; the tool below lists what did change besides the structure. Two things: the step sizes now come from vac_steps, which gives the same Δp and Δα as the replaced lines for every case that runs (they differ only for nα = 1, which calc_GH_1 refuses), and the CHECKVAC report of the diagonals names vac_diag_style and says "assembled" instead of "rowsum". So the split is a restructure; in appendix A it moves the nine eigenvalues by at most 1.4e-13. What changed inside the steps, against 2.47:

  • set_up_data: EP = −(nq − m) times cos and sin of the phase, the normal data along −J∇ψ (2.47: +). The phase is nα + (nq − m)p on the field lines and −mθ for the axisymmetric style.
  • project_response: for the field-line style the weight is Δp / nα (halved at the line ends): the projection averages over the lines, which matches the per-toroidal-mode normalisation of the axisymmetric style. 2.47 used Δp Δα.
  • finish_response: res = (2π/μ₀) times the complex combination of the four real blocks, then its Hermitian part. The 2π: A holds 2δW with the toroidal integral included, and res up to there is per unit toroidal angle. The Hermitian part: the discretisation leaves an asymmetry of about 1e-5, which made SLEPc reject converged eigenvalues as complex.

How the factor was found. Commit 212e3ec (27 October) measured it before writing it: on the circular ε = 0.05 external kink a factor of magnitude 2π reproduced MISHKA-1 without a wall to 0.3 to 0.8 % at n = 10, 15 and 20, while 1 and 4π were 60 to 80 % off; then it was derived (the comment in finish_response). The sign was −2π then, with the data sign of that time; since f3ebf11 the data are −(nq − m) and the factor is +2π, with the same response.

Check of the factor: in the large-aspect-ratio cylinder the vacuum energy of an edge displacement equals the field-line bending energy of a plasma layer of thickness a/|m| at the edge, 4π²(nq − m)²|Xe|²/(μ₀R₀|m|) in A. The full-stack test vac_response_cylinder checks res(m, m) against it (1 to 2 % for m = 1 … 5). The external kink against MISHKA-1 and TERPSICHORE is the end-to-end check of the sign and the factor: circular ε = 0.33, n = 10, free boundary: PB3D's field-line style at 128 lines × 400 points −0.438488, TERPSICHORE −0.438480 (with its kinetic-energy parity error corrected), MISHKA-1 −0.43377 (1.1 % less unstable, probably its parallel inertia; not verified).

How to check it
  1. In set_up_data: the sign of EP, and the phase for each style.
  2. In project_response: the weight Δp/nα with the halved line ends, and the axisymmetric weight (θi+1 − θi−1)/2.
  3. In finish_response: the combination of the four real blocks into the complex res (the sign comment for the lower rows is from 2.47), then /μ₀, ×2π, Hermitian part.

E

Reading guide to the wave report

file Documentation/vacuum_wave_report/report.pdf on branch exp/vacuum-wave-prototype (worktree .claude/worktrees/vacuum-wave-prototype), 7 pages, 4 Novembertime 1 to 2 hstudy

Where the cost is now. The field-line vacuum solves for the potential at every boundary point, with two dense N × N matrices G and H and a dense LU (memory N², work N³). A Richardson level doubles the points per line, so N doubles and the matrices grow fourfold. At 128 lines × 400 points (N = 51 200, the grid that matches TERPSICHORE on the circular twin to 2e-5) one matrix takes 21 GB and the run was given 220 GB.

What the report proposes (your idea, prototyped in Python on 3 November): write the potential as a sum of M′ waves ei(nφ − mkθ) and require the error of the boundary equation to have no component along any of those waves (Galerkin). Then the unknowns are M′ amplitudes, the system is M′ × M′, and its entries are sums over pairs of points. Under Richardson those sums carry over: a pair of old points enters with ¼ of its previous value, and only pairs with a new point are computed. No N × N matrix is ever stored.

Read in this order:

  1. Abstract and §I (1 page): the problem and the claim.
  2. §II, eqs. (1)–(5): the point-value form, which is what calc_GH_1 does now. Careful with signs: the report was written on 3 and 4 November, before the one convention of 5 November. Its n points out of the plasma, its Hij = −wjK, and its self term Hii = 2π − ci. The code now takes the normal into the plasma (V0), which flips the signs of H and of the data together. The method does not depend on the choice.
  3. Fig. 2 and §III.A: why waves in the geometric angle need sidebands. Each exact harmonic of the torus is spread over a band of m (3 to 10 for m = 6, 9 to 24 for m = 15), because the geometric and the toroidal-coordinate poloidal angles differ by up to 0.345 rad at aspect ratio 3.
  4. §III.B–C, eqs. (6)–(11), and Fig. 3: the wave form; A is nearly tridiagonal (first neighbours 0.17 of the diagonal, a/(2R₀) = 1/6).
  5. §IV, eqs. (12)–(15), and Fig. 4: the Richardson step. Compare with §VI of your 2019 manuscript: option B there (reuse of the LU of H) is wrong as written (erratum E12); the wave form is a fourth option, closest to option C.
  6. Table I and §V: the four criteria fixed before the runs, all met: carried-over sums equal sums from scratch to 9e-16; the energy error equals the point-value form's to 3 digits; 34 waves for a 10-mode window; 51 264 points in 1.9 GB.
  7. §VI: what is not tested. This list decides whether the method can enter PB3D: the near-field rule (near pairs change when points are added), a stellarator (waves n ± Nfp, …), PB3D's own data (single waves, the case of Fig. 5(c)-like data: 3 to 4 sidebands would do), the straight-field-line angle, Fortran and MPI.

Where it meets the code. The rows are those of calc_GH_1 without the near-field rule; the data b correspond to EP of set_up_data; the energy (eq. 11) to project_response. Your 2018 \todo in the header of calc_GH (reuse of the contributions of inherited points, scaled by ½) and interlaced_vac_copy are the point-value precursor of eq. (14).

The other options, all exploration only and nothing decided (overview, section 7): a doubly periodic (θ, ζ) vacuum grid separate from the field-line grid; locally corrected trapezoidal quadrature on such a grid (Wu–Martinsson, Alpert, Duan–Rokhlin, Malhotra); a Fourier-basis vacuum as in CAS3D/NESTOR; a fast solver (GMRES with FMM or H-matrix products, or a HODLR direct solve; the condition number is only 3.5 to 3.8). The gate for any of them: the check_vac exact potentials on the circular twin and on W7-X 128 × 121 must improve and the twin eigenvalue must stay within its regression tolerance. The parked next step for the wave form (issue #4 comment of 3 November): W7-X from ~/Code/desc-vs-vmec/overnight/inputs/w7x_2015/wout_w7x.03.00llfix_99.nc, port the near-field rule, reproduce check_vac at 128 × 121, then compare wave form and point values, then the carry-over.

What else the table tells you.

  • At the port every HELENA run, fixed boundary included, stops in store_vac_HEL with "Vacuum has not been implemented yet!". The vacuum had been switched off this way since 2.44 (commit 53c1c58, 18 November 2020; the message of ad2313b says the same). So the first HELENA eigenvalue in the table is at ad2313b, which skips the vacuum when the boundary is fixed.
  • Between 1d74b8f (style 2 implemented) and 212e3ec, the circular deck with a free boundary gave the fixed-boundary value to 8e-8 (+7.76499e-2, a stable mode), with the factor that the vacuum response then had. 212e3ec set the factor to −2π (its message gives the derivation and the MISHKA check), and the same deck went to −0.430.
  • The VMEC twin with a free boundary changed at six commits: −0.630 at 1d74b8f, −0.0660 after the factor (212e3ec), −0.0833 after the field-line spacing (50c9511), −0.527 after the inward normal (e3b4847), −0.519 after B0 (0d49f19), −0.449 after the near-field rule and the G diagonal (0bbda47), and −0.439 after the edge weight (4915d4b). The axisymmetric (style 2) vacuum gives −0.43679 for the HELENA version of the same equilibrium (comment of the twin regression test).

What the table cannot see. (1) Whether the port changed a number, because 2.47 does not build against PETSc 3.25. (2) The MPI fix 798fe50 (F3), because every run uses one process; F3's evidence is the comparison of 1, 2 and 4 processes. (3) The vacuum commits before style 2 worked (429f003, d6d49e6): no BC-2 deck gave an eigenvalue then, and the BC-4 deck uses only the axisymmetric vacuum. (4) Much of the 3-D vacuum on W7-X: at 16 field lines and n = 10, the W7-X mode hardly feels the vacuum. At the tip, free and fixed boundary differ by 1.8e-4, and no vacuum commit moves the free value by more than 1.2e-4. So the W7-X columns check the VMEC path with ntor > 0 more than the vacuum, and not even the Nyquist matching, because this file's two mode lists coincide (the 6.4 % at 0d49f19 is B0). A rippled twin with ntor 6 would be the deck that shows the Nyquist fix.
