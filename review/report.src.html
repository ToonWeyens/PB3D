Terms used in this page (open once)
field-line style, axisymmetric style
The two vacuum methods, vac%style 1 and 2. Style 1 (VMEC) is the 3-D boundary-element method on the points of the field lines; style 2 (HELENA) integrates over ζ analytically with toroidal functions and over θ numerically.
BC_style
Boundary conditions; its second value is the plasma edge: 1 fixed, 2 free through the variational surface energy, 4 free through an explicit natural-boundary-condition row.
vac_diag_style
How the diagonals of the vacuum matrices G and H are computed: 1 indirect (from identities), 2 direct (corner term and cell integral). New input.
X, p
X = ξ·∇ψ, PB3D's normal displacement; p the parallel angle along a field line (θ for poloidal flux). A mode: X ∝ ei(nα + (nq−m)p).
EP, res
EP: the normal data of the vacuum potential for each mode at the boundary points (in calc_vac_res). res = vac%res, the nmod × nmod vacuum response that enters the edge block of A; res(m, m) its diagonal element for poloidal mode m.
lines × points
A field-line vacuum grid: number of field lines × points per line, e.g. 128 × 121 on W7-X.
the circular cases
HELENA circular-cross-section equilibria with q from 1.04 (fixtures circ_eps005_q104, circ_eps033_q104, inverse aspect ratio 0.05 and 0.33); the n = 10 external kink is the free-boundary benchmark.
VMEC twin, rippled twin
The VMEC equilibrium made from the ε = 0.33 HELENA case (same plasma, VMEC++), and copies of it with a 5-fold ripple of amplitude δ (0.01 to 0.04): the 3-D tests of the field-line style.
cbm18a, cut cbm18a
The HELENA pedestal equilibrium of the regression decks (n = 10, edge at ψ = 0.75 in the free-boundary deck), and a version of it cut at the pedestal foot for the comparison with TERPSICHORE.
MISHKA-1, TERPSICHORE
The axisymmetric code you used in your PhD (rebuilt from Simulations/MISHKA/Sources), and the open-source 3-D stability code of EPFL (since 2025), run on Snellius.
check_vac, CHECKVAC
New input that makes PB3D test its vacuum operators with exact potentials on the real boundary; the results are log lines starting with CHECKVAC.
anchor, fixture
An eigenvalue recorded in a regression test, and an equilibrium file committed for the tests (tests/fixtures).
eigenvalue
ω²/ωA² in PB3D's MISHKA normalisation (norm_style 1), negative when unstable.
Snellius
The SURF cluster; the runs of 27 October on were made there.

M1

PETSc/SLEPc 3.9 to 3.25 port

files SLEPC_ops, SLEPC_utilitiescommits 775219a, d4812f7, 1862df0time 15 minread

2.47 used the PETSc 3.9 Fortran interface and no longer builds against a current PETSc. The port changes calls, not what is computed: PetscBool optional arguments become logical, InsertMode gets its own type, MatSetValuesBlocked takes index arrays and a flattened block, PetscViewerSetFormat becomes push/pop, VecGetArrayF90 becomes VecGetArray, and MatInfo and PETSC_NULL_SCALAR_ARRAY are switched on PETSC_VERSION_GE(3,22,0).

Two hunks are more than renames:

  • An implicit SAVE, fixed. In the routine that removes faulty eigenvalues, 2.47 declared PetscBool :: remove_next_loc = .false.. An initialisation in a declaration gives the variable the SAVE attribute, so after one call with remove_next = .true. every later call without the argument also removed all following eigenvalues. The port sets it to .false. at the start of each call. The demo shows the Fortran rule. (Effect by reading of the code: it only matters in runs where PB3D discards eigenvalues; I did not construct such a case.)
  • MatCopy flag. 2.47 passed MAT_SHARE_NONZERO_PATTERN (a MatDuplicateOption) where a MatStructure is expected; the port passes SAME_NONZERO_PATTERN. In petscmat.h of PETSc 3.25.3 both names are the third entry of their enumeration (value 2), so this corrects the type, not the behaviour.
How to check it
  1. Read the code below the three cards; in their own diffs the classifier marks three hunks as mechanical (two renames of PetscBool, one debug guard); the rest are API changes to read once.
  2. The build at the tip and the regression anchors (S0) show that the ported calls work; 2.47 cannot be built with PETSc 3.25, so there is no before/after run of the port itself.

F1

HELENA shear: the end condition of an odd spline

file eq_ops (calc_derived_q)commit 28f8dabtime 20 minstudy

For a HELENA equilibrium PB3D computes the magnetic shear as \(S = -\frac{1}{\mathcal{J}}\,\partial_\chi\!\left(h^{\alpha\psi}/h^{\psi\psi}\right)\) by fitting a cubic spline in χ and differentiating it (calc_derived_S_from_deriv_HEL). An up-down symmetric equilibrium (ias = 0) is stored on χ ∈ [0, π], so the spline needs a condition at both ends. The ratio \(h^{\alpha\psi}/h^{\psi\psi}\) is odd in χ: its slope is not zero at χ = 0 and π, its second derivative is. 2.47 passed bcs(:,1), the zero-slope condition of even quantities. The fix passes bcs(:,2), zero second derivative. One line; the other three hunks correct the comment "zero first derivative" next to bcs(:,2) = [2,2] in three places.

Effect. The zero-slope condition forced S to zero at both midplanes, χ = 0 and π. The cbm18a n = 10 fixed-boundary eigenvalue moved from −4.074e-2 to −4.357e-2, 6.5 % more unstable, and POST's energy balance closes to 0.08 %. The faulty argument dates from 18 June 2018 (b8c07f7, v2.36) and is in the code of the tag Weyens2018expressions. The demo shows that the error at the ends does not shrink with the number of poloidal points.

How to check it
  1. Read the change: bcs(:,1) → bcs(:,2) in the call for eq_2%S (below the card).
  2. Look at the demo: with the even condition the derivative error stays 0.86 at the ends for 33 to 257 points; with the odd one it falls as 1/N⁴.
  3. Independent evidence: the VMEC path does not use this spline, and the HELENA and VMEC versions of the circular ε = 0.33 case agree to 1e-4 in their fixed-boundary eigenvalue (test regression_circ_eps033_vmec_twin, TWIN_RTOL).
  4. Guard: regression_cbm18a_shear_HEL compares the shear S in the output file with the direct formula from the stored metric derivatives: deviation 0.79 with the even condition, 1.5e-5 with the odd one.
F2

setup_modes: table slot from the mode number

file X_ops (setup_modes)commit e4e3fddtime 20 minread

With the fast mode window (X_style 2) the window of n_mod_X poloidal modes follows nq across the radius, and modes end and start where the window moves. PB3D keeps two tables that must agree: nm_X (mode number per radial point and slot) and ind_tot (per mode: number, radial range, slot). 2.47 computed the slot of a new mode from the slot of the mode created last, which agrees with nm_X only while the window moves up. When it moves down (nq < 0), reverses (reversed shear) or jitters (a flat q), two active modes shared a slot and their couplings were mixed without a message. That formula dates from 27 March 2018 (63ce0f4, v2.14). The fix takes the slot from the mode number with the formula nm_X uses, modulo(m - nm_X(1,1), n_mod_X) + 1, and adds a check that every mode finds its own number in its slot at every point of its range (a new runtime error if not).

The script tests/reference/setup_modes_sim.py copies the index formulas of 2.47, of an unmerged 2020 rewrite (afc9d17 on origin/issue_vac_not_used_VMEC), of a corrected version of that rewrite, and of the fix, and counts wrong (point, mode) entries for eight window histories. It runs in ctest as unit_setup_modes_sim.

How to check it
  1. Read the two changed formulas against nm_X's own rule, modulo(m - nm_X(1,1), n_mod_X) + 1.
  2. Read the new check loop after the main loop.
  3. Run or read the sim: the column "current" (the code as at 2.47; the script was written before the fix) has wrong entries for five of the eight window histories, the column "fixed" none.
Gap. None of the fixtures moves the window down, so no eigenvalue changed and no regression test covers the case. The sim tests a Python copy of the formulas, not the Fortran; the Fortran's own check is the new loop, which stops a run whose tables disagree.
F3

MPI: every point from its owner

files MPI_utilities (redistribute_var), grid_opscommit 798fe50time 25 minstudy

With 2 or 4 MPI processes every eigenvalue differed from the 1-process one by about 2e-5 relative. Cause: every process hands its whole local range to redistribute_var, ghost points included, so the source ranges overlap. 2.47 filled each destination point from the sources in rank order, which in an overlap takes the lower rank's ghost region, where derivatives are one-sided and splines end. A and B differed from the 1-process matrices in the 12 to 16 rows after each interface, by 6e-6 and 2e-5 of their largest entry. The fix splits each overlap in the middle (the convention of trim_grid) and takes every point from its owner. On Snellius the cbm18a fixed deck then gives the same eigenvalue with 1, 2, 3 and 4 processes to 1.5e-8, the circular deck to 2e-12. The rank-order loop dates from 22 March 2017 (2bc8bbc, v1.61).

A second, smaller part: grid_ops widens the equilibrium range by 20 points (ghost_interp), because interp_V fits a cubic spline over exactly the local range and a spline's end effect reaches inward, falling by about 2 − √3 ≈ 0.27 per interval. Its contribution to the eigenvalue was 1e-6. The right panel of the demo shows the decay.

How to check it
  1. Run or read the demo: it copies both versions of the index arithmetic and marks the eight points (of 60, with 3 processes and 4 ghost points) that 2.47 took from a ghost region.
  2. Read the new loop against the demo: lims_eff_tot is the owned range, id_start and n_vars the intersection of destination and owned range, and id_rec/id_sen the offsets into the local arrays.
  3. Guard: regression_cbm18a_fixed_bnd_np2 and _np4 (tolerance 1e-6 against one process). They are opt-in (-DPB3D_TEST_MPI_REGRESSION=ON, needs MUMPS in PETSc); they ran on Snellius, and CI does not run them.
F4

VMEC: the Nyquist mode list and B0

file VMEC_ops (read_VMEC)commit 0d49f19time 20 minread

Mode list. A VMEC wout file stores R, Z and λ on the main mode list (xm, xn, mnmax modes) and the Jacobian, |B| and the covariant B components on the Nyquist list (xm_nyq, xn_nyq, mnmax_nyq ≥ mnmax), each ordered by its own limits. 2.47 took gmnc(1:mnmax,:), bmnc(1:mnmax,:) and bsub*(1:mnmax,:) and labelled them with the main list. That is right only when ntor = 0 or mnmax_nyq = mnmax (the STELLOPT W7-X file). For a VMEC++ file of the rippled twin (mpol 12, ntor 6) 143 of 150 labels were wrong, and the fixed-boundary eigenvalue was 37 % off. Commit 0d49f19 matched the modes by (m, n); the Fourier-basis commit 3b99109 (R1) then kept the whole Nyquist list. The line as at 2.47 dates from July 2017 (ca8a070, v1.86).

B0. PB3D normalises with |B| on the axis. 2.47 summed all bmnc modes, which is |B| at θ = ζ = 0 and depends on where the toroidal origin lies; on a rippled axis it varies with the ripple amplitude δ, so the normalised eigenvalues varied linearly in δ while the physics is even in δ. Now B0 is the (0, 0) Nyquist mode, the toroidal average. Even on the axisymmetric twin the old B0 was 0.8 % low: the HELENA and VMEC fixed-boundary eigenvalues of the circular twin were 1.6 % apart and now agree to 2e-6. On the rippled twins the eigenvalues now change as δ² (ratios 4.0 and 3.9 fixed, 4.1 and 4.9 free, for doubling δ); W7-X eigenvalues rescale by 1.064. The sum dates from April 2017 (df0f239, v1.67).

How to check it
  1. Run or read the demo: it builds both mode lists with VMEC's ordering and counts the mislabelled entries, and checks the claim on the committed fixture (ntor 0: no mislabelling).
  2. Read the B0 loop and the Nyquist block of read_VMEC; every 1:mnmax_V on a Nyquist array became 1:mnmax_nyq_V.
Gap. No committed test has a VMEC file with ntor > 0: the only VMEC fixture, wout_circ_eps033_q104.nc, is axisymmetric, so neither the Nyquist fix, the B0 fix nor the Nyquist path of the Fourier basis (R1) is guarded in CI. The rippled twins and W7-X were run on Snellius.
F5

Free edge: the trapezoidal end weight

file SLEPC_ops (fill_mat)commit 4915d4btime 25 minstudy

fill_mat adds the energy integrand of each radial point times the step size. With a fixed boundary the last row is replaced by the boundary condition, so the weight of the last point never enters. With a free boundary (BC_style(2) = 2 or 4) the last point stays in the system and the trapezoidal rule gives it half the weight. 2.47 gave it the full weight, so the free-boundary eigenvalues converged like 1/N in n_r_sol (2.3 % off at the 200 points of the regression decks on the HELENA circular ε = 0.33 kink: −0.44383 before, −0.43380 after, comment of the twin regression test) while fixed-boundary ones converged like 1/N². The fix multiplies the blocks of the last point by w_q = 1/2 for those two styles.

Data flow

The convention since 5 November (f3ebf11)

Both vacuum styles now use one convention, the vacuum's. The stored normal is \(\boldsymbol{n} = -\mathcal{J}\nabla\psi\): it points into the plasma, which is out of the vacuum region, and its length carries the surface element, \(\mathrm{d}S = |\mathcal{J}\nabla\psi|\,\mathrm{d}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\alpha\). With the normal data \(\phi' = \boldsymbol{n}\cdot\nabla\phi\) the discretised operators are

\[ G_{ij} \approx -\frac{w_j}{|\boldsymbol{x}_i-\boldsymbol{x}_j|}, \qquad H_{ij} \approx w_j\,\frac{\boldsymbol{n}_j\cdot(\boldsymbol{x}_j-\boldsymbol{x}_i)}{|\boldsymbol{x}_j-\boldsymbol{x}_i|^3} \quad (i\neq j), \]

with the quadrature weights \(w_j\) (field lines: Δp Δα / 2 per half interval; the axisymmetric style integrates over ζ analytically with the toroidal functions \(Q_{|n|-1/2}\)). For a point \(\boldsymbol{x}_i\) on the boundary and the diagonal included, the assembled operators satisfy

\[ \sum_j H_{ij}\phi_j - \sum_j G_{ij}\phi'_j = \begin{cases} 0 & \phi \text{ harmonic in the vacuum, decaying,}\\ -4\pi\,\phi_i & \phi \text{ harmonic in the plasma.}\end{cases} \]

The second line with φ = 1 gives \(\sum_j H_{ij} = -4\pi\), the row-sum rule that sets the field-line H diagonal (your design at 2.47). On a smooth surface the diagonal is \(H_{ii} = -2\pi\) plus the integral of the H kernel over the cell; at a corner it is \(-2\beta\) with β the vacuum's internal angle (β = π on a smooth surface), which is what calc_GH_2 adds. The exterior problem is then \(H\Phi = G\,\phi'\), solved without any shift of the diagonal for both styles.

The normal data come from the plasma displacement. With X = ξ·∇ψ and p the parallel angle, continuity of the normal field at the boundary gives \(\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\phi = \boldsymbol{B}\cdot\nabla X\), and \(\mathcal{J}\boldsymbol{B}\cdot\nabla = \partial/\partial p\) along a field line in PB3D's flux coordinates, so \(\phi' = -\mathcal{J}\nabla\psi\cdot\nabla\phi = -\partial X/\partial p\), which for one mode \(X \propto e^{i(n\alpha + (nq-m)p)}\) gives \(-i(nq-m)X\). That is the factor \(-(nq-m)\) in set_up_data; the imaginary unit sits in the pairing of cos and sin columns. With this sign the vacuum energy \(\delta W_v = \frac{1}{2\mu_0}\oint \phi'^*\phi\,\mathrm{d}S\) is positive, and finish_response multiplies by 2π (the toroidal integral, since A carries 2δW including it) and divides by μ₀.

Derivation status: the identities and their constants follow from Green's second identity with this normal, and the data from the boundary condition above; I derived both for this page, and the toy below checks the identities numerically. The same convention is described in the commit message of f3ebf11. The code's own checks are the full-stack tests greens_identity_3d, exact_exterior_2d and vac_response_cylinder (T).

Reading guide to the wave report

file Documentation/vacuum_wave_report/report.pdf on branch exp/vacuum-wave-prototype (worktree .claude/worktrees/vacuum-wave-prototype), 7 pages, 4 Novembertime 1 to 2 hstudy

Where the cost is now. The field-line vacuum solves for the potential at every boundary point, with two dense N × N matrices G and H and a dense LU (memory N², work N³). A Richardson level doubles the points per line, so N doubles and the matrices grow fourfold. At 128 lines × 400 points (N = 51 200, the grid that matches TERPSICHORE on the circular twin to 2e-5) one matrix takes 21 GB and the run was given 220 GB.

What the report proposes (your idea, prototyped in Python on 3 November): write the potential as a sum of M′ waves ei(nφ − mkθ) and require the error of the boundary equation to have no component along any of those waves (Galerkin). Then the unknowns are M′ amplitudes, the system is M′ × M′, and its entries are sums over pairs of points. Under Richardson those sums carry over: a pair of old points enters with ¼ of its previous value, and only pairs with a new point are computed. No N × N matrix is ever stored.

Read in this order:

  1. Abstract and §I (1 page): the problem and the claim.
  2. §II, eqs. (1)–(5): the point-value form, which is what calc_GH_1 does now. Careful with signs: the report was written on 3 and 4 November, before the one convention of 5 November. Its n points out of the plasma, its Hij = −wjK, and its self term Hii = 2π − ci. The code now takes the normal into the plasma (V0), which flips the signs of H and of the data together. The method does not depend on the choice.
  3. Fig. 2 and §III.A: why waves in the geometric angle need sidebands. Each exact harmonic of the torus is spread over a band of m (3 to 10 for m = 6, 9 to 24 for m = 15), because the geometric and the toroidal-coordinate poloidal angles differ by up to 0.345 rad at aspect ratio 3.
  4. §III.B–C, eqs. (6)–(11), and Fig. 3: the wave form; A is nearly tridiagonal (first neighbours 0.17 of the diagonal, a/(2R₀) = 1/6).
  5. §IV, eqs. (12)–(15), and Fig. 4: the Richardson step. Compare with §VI of your 2019 manuscript: option B there (reuse of the LU of H) is wrong as written (erratum E12); the wave form is a fourth option, closest to option C.
  6. Table I and §V: the four criteria fixed before the runs, all met: carried-over sums equal sums from scratch to 9e-16; the energy error equals the point-value form's to 3 digits; 34 waves for a 10-mode window; 51 264 points in 1.9 GB.
  7. §VI: what is not tested. This list decides whether the method can enter PB3D: the near-field rule (near pairs change when points are added), a stellarator (waves n ± Nfp, …), PB3D's own data (single waves, the case of Fig. 5(c)-like data: 3 to 4 sidebands would do), the straight-field-line angle, Fortran and MPI.

Where it meets the code. The rows are those of calc_GH_1 without the near-field rule; the data b correspond to EP of set_up_data; the energy (eq. 11) to project_response. Your 2018 \todo in the header of calc_GH (reuse of the contributions of inherited points, scaled by ½) and interlaced_vac_copy are the point-value precursor of eq. (14).

The other options, all exploration only and nothing decided (overview, section 7): a doubly periodic (θ, ζ) vacuum grid separate from the field-line grid; locally corrected trapezoidal quadrature on such a grid (Wu–Martinsson, Alpert, Duan–Rokhlin, Malhotra); a Fourier-basis vacuum as in CAS3D/NESTOR; a fast solver (GMRES with FMM or H-matrix products, or a HODLR direct solve; the condition number is only 3.5 to 3.8). The gate for any of them: the check_vac exact potentials on the circular twin and on W7-X 128 × 121 must improve and the twin eigenvalue must stay within its regression tolerance. The parked next step for the wave form (issue #4 comment of 3 November): W7-X from ~/Code/desc-vs-vmec/overnight/inputs/w7x_2015/wout_w7x.03.00llfix_99.nc, port the near-field rule, reproduce check_vac at 128 × 121, then compare wave form and point values, then the carry-over.
