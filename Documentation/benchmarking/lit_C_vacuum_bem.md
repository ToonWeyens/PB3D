## 0. Corrections to the brief

- **J. Comput. Phys. 221 (2007) 330** by Chance, Turnbull and Snyder is titled *"Calculation of the vacuum Green's function valid even for high toroidal mode numbers in tokamaks"*. It is an axisymmetric paper on evaluating the toroidal Fourier components G_n of 1/|x−x'| at high n. It does not treat 3-D surfaces [read]. I found no Chance paper titled "Calculating the vacuum response in three dimensions" and no public code called VACUUM3D. The public 3-D descendant of VACUUM is the 2026 Julia rewrite inside GPEC (entry 1.3).
- Malhotra et al., "Efficient high-order singular quadrature schemes in magnetic fusion" is PPCF **62** (2020) 024004; Crossref gives 2019 as the online date.
- Strumberger & Günter, CASTOR3D: Nucl. Fusion **57** (2017) 016032 (online 2016).

## 1. Vacuum calculations in MHD stability and equilibrium codes

### 1.1 Chance 1997, VACUUM in axisymmetric geometry [read]
M. S. Chance, "Vacuum calculations in azimuthally symmetric geometry", *Phys. Plasmas* **4**, 2161–2180 (1997). DOI [10.1063/1.872380](https://doi.org/10.1063/1.872380). Preprint PPPL-3216: https://bp-pub.pppl.gov/pub_report/1997/PPPL-3216.pdf . The published PDF is also stored in the GPEC Julia repository, `docs/resources/`: https://github.com/OpenFUSIONToolkit/GPEC/tree/develop/docs/resources

Green's second identity with collocation on the plasma contour (and wall contours); the kernel is the toroidal Fourier component of 1/|x−x'|. Near each collocation point θ_j the unknown is interpolated by 5-point Lagrange polynomials on [θ_j−2Δ, θ_j+2Δ]; the log singularity of G is subtracted and integrated analytically against these polynomials (eqs. 75–78), and the smooth remainder is integrated with 8-point Gauss–Legendre on each side. Away from the singular region the weights are trapezoidal for periodic contours and Simpson otherwise. The double-layer self-term ("residue", eq. 81 and Table I) comes from integrating Laplace's equation for the n = 0 Green's function over the vacuum region, which is the constant-potential identity PB3D uses for the H diagonal. Reported accuracy: "within 10^-5 easily achievable" for a simple closed wall; the field outside a closed shell vanishes to about 10^-7 of interior values; the symmetry of the vacuum matrices is checked routinely as an internal accuracy test.

### 1.2 Chance, Turnbull, Snyder 2007, the Green's function at high n [read]
M. S. Chance, A. D. Turnbull, P. B. Snyder, "Calculation of the vacuum Green's function valid even for high toroidal mode numbers in tokamaks", *J. Comput. Phys.* **221**, 330–348 (2007). DOI [10.1016/j.jcp.2006.06.025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2006.06.025). Preprint PPPL-4152: https://bp-pub.pppl.gov/pub_report/2006/PPPL-4152.pdf

The upward recursion in n from the elliptic integrals K and E (used in GATO, ERATO, PEST) loses all digits at moderate n. The paper instead integrates the integral representation of G_n directly: it subtracts the log singularity, changes variable, and uses the trapezoid rule on the periodic integrand; for large n·ρ̂ it deforms the contour into the complex φ plane and uses Gauss quadrature. It reaches 12–16 digits and gives the relation to the associated Legendre functions P^n_{-1/2}. *(my reading)*: useful for PB3D only as a cross-check of the axisymmetric G_n (dtorh) at high n.

### 1.3 VACUUM inside GPEC: Fortran source and the 2026 Julia 3-D version [read]
- **Fortran**: https://github.com/PrincetonUniversity/GPEC has a `vacuum/` directory. Release v1.6.0 (https://github.com/PrincetonUniversity/GPEC/releases/tag/v1.6.0) says GPEC is now under the MIT license and makes the Chance 2007 Green's function the default (`use_legacy_greens_function` restores the old one). So the Fortran VACUUM source is public.
- **Julia**: https://github.com/OpenFUSIONToolkit/GPEC (MIT; `src/Vacuum/Kernel3D.jl` is Apache-2.0, translated from BIEST). Docs: https://openfusiontoolkit.github.io/GPEC/dev/vacuum/ . I read `Kernel3D.jl`, `Vacuum.jl` and `test/runtests_vacuum.jl`. The 3-D path takes Cartesian x, y, z on a regular (θ, ζ) grid and builds the single-layer (1/r) and double-layer (n'·∇'(1/r)) matrices: far field by the plain trapezoid rule on the collocation grid, near field by Malhotra's polar quadrature (patch radius 11 grid points, 20 radial Gauss points, 5th-order Lagrange interpolation, partition of unity exp(−36 r^p)). Rows are divided by 2π and the free term is added as a unit diagonal (the H diagonal comes from the quadrature, not from a row-sum identity). Field periods are folded by a block-circulant reduction over k = n mod nfp, and stellarator symmetry halves the blocks. W_v = (4π²/M) E^H (D^{-1} S) E as in Chance 1997. In 3-D only "nowall" and "conformal" walls are allowed.
  Tests: on a circular torus (R0 = 1.7, a = 0.3, 48 × 96 grid) the 3-D W_v agrees with the 2-D Chance path to better than 1e-3 (relative Frobenius norm). The stellarator tests (rotating ellipse, nfp = 3) check symmetry handling and field-period folding, not accuracy against an exact answer.
- **Conference abstract** [read]: J. Halpern, N. Logan, E. Paul, C. Paz-Soldan, M. Pharr, J. Cho, J. Lee, P. Lunia, D. Burgess, B. Lyons, Sherwood Theory Conference 2026, https://sherwoodtheory.org/sw2026/abstract.php?view=247 . Open-source Julia code for the vacuum energy response matrix in tokamaks and stellarators; reproduces the Fortran 2-D results; an axisymmetric case discretised as a 3-D surface agrees with the 2-D matrix to "less than 0.1%"; for a stellarator only convergence with toroidal resolution is shown.
- *(my reading)*: this is the only public 3-D vacuum-response code for stability that I found, and its output (a vacuum energy matrix from a Chance-style collocation formulation) is the closest to what PB3D computes.

### 1.5 Merkel 1986, NESTOR [meta; method read in two secondary sources]
P. Merkel, "An integral equation technique for the exterior and interior Neumann problem in toroidal regions", *J. Comput. Phys.* **66**, 83–98 (1986). DOI [10.1016/0021-9991(86)90055-0](https://doi.org/10.1016/0021-9991(86)90055-0).
Companions: S. P. Hirshman, W. I. van Rij, P. Merkel, "Three-dimensional free boundary calculations using a spectral Green's function method", *Comput. Phys. Commun.* **43**, 143–155 (1986), DOI [10.1016/0010-4655(86)90058-5](https://doi.org/10.1016/0010-4655(86)90058-5) [meta]; P. Merkel, "Applications of the Neumann problem to stellarators: magnetic surfaces, coils, free-boundary equilibrium, magnetic diagnostics", in *Theory of Fusion Plasmas* (Varenna 1988), pp. 25–46, http://hdl.handle.net/11858/00-001M-0000-0028-BDF5-4 [snippet, cited in the VMEC++ notes as a corrected version of the 1986 paper].

From Malhotra et al. 2020 and Schilling 2026 (both read): NESTOR writes Green's identity on the surface, Φ + (1/2π)∫ ∂G/∂n' Φ dS' = (1/2π)∫ G ∂Φ/∂n' dS', and solves for Φ in a truncated double Fourier basis. It subtracts surrogate kernels built from the first fundamental form at the target, with (u−u') and (v−v') replaced by tan(π(u−u'))/π and tan(π(v−v'))/π so that the surrogate stays doubly periodic, and computes the Fourier transforms of the surrogates analytically; the remainder is summed with the trapezoid rule. Malhotra et al. (Fig. 2) show that this converges only as O(h²) on the constant-potential Green's-identity test, on a circular torus and on W7-X, because the subtracted integrand is bounded but not smooth at the target.

### 1.6 Schilling 2026, "The Numerics of VMEC++" (NESTOR re-derived) [read]
J. Schilling, "The Numerics of VMEC++", arXiv:2502.04374v3 (Feb. 2026), https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.04374 . Code: https://github.com/proximafusion/vmecpp (MIT, C++ and Python).
Chapter 6 re-derives NESTOR: problem statement (6.1–6.5), subtraction of singular surrogates (Sec. 6.3.4, eqs. 6.67–6.68), analytic Fourier transform of the singular coefficients (Sec. 6.3.5), the linear system in tangential Fourier space (Sec. 6.4.6), and the axisymmetric variant (Sec. 6.5). This is the clearest account of NESTOR's singularity treatment that I found; VMEC++ contains a C++ NESTOR.

## 2. High-order boundary-integral solvers on toroidal surfaces

### 2.1 BIEST paper, Taylor states [read: abstract and docs]
D. Malhotra, A. Cerfon, L.-M. Imbert-Gérard, M. O'Neil, "Taylor states in stellarators: a fast high-order boundary integral solver", *J. Comput. Phys.* **397**, 108791 (2019). DOI [10.1016/j.jcp.2019.06.067](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2019.06.067). arXiv:1902.01205, https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01205 .
Generalized Debye source representation, second-kind boundary integral equation, spectral discretisation on a uniform (θ, φ) grid, partition-of-unity polar quadrature for the weakly singular integrals, GMRES with preconditioning. Search snippet only: benchmarked against SPEC on a W7-X boundary to about 9 digits.

### 2.2 Malhotra et al. 2020, singular quadrature for fusion [read: full text]
D. Malhotra, A. J. Cerfon, M. O'Neil, E. Toler, "Efficient high-order singular quadrature schemes in magnetic fusion", *Plasma Phys. Control. Fusion* **62**, 024004 (2020). DOI [10.1088/1361-6587/ab57f4](https://doi.org/10.1088/1361-6587/ab57f4). arXiv:1909.07417, https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.07417 .
A partition of unity χ splits the kernel into a C∞ far part (trapezoid rule, spectral convergence) and a compactly supported near part, which is integrated in local polar coordinates (trapezoid in angle, Gauss–Legendre in radius) after Lagrange interpolation of geometry and density from the grid; the polar Jacobian cancels the 1/r singularity. On the constant-potential identity 1 + (1/2π)∫ n'·(x−x')/|x−x'|³ dS' = 0, Merkel's subtraction converges as O(h²) while the new scheme converges at roughly 12th order on a circular torus and 10th order on W7-X (Fig. 2). The exterior Neumann problem is written as the second-kind equation (2.7) and solved with GMRES; W7-X tests converge to about 2e-10 (Table 1, virtual casing of a Taylor state, 392 × 1960 grid) and 1e-9 (Table 2, vacuum field plus virtual casing with current loops). The paper calls accurate evaluation near but off the surface an open problem.

### 2.3 BIEST code [read: README and documentation]
https://github.com/dmalhotra/BIEST (Apache-2.0; header-only C++17 with OpenMP; optional FFTW, BLAS/LAPACK, LIBXSMM). Docs: https://biest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/
- Laplace single layer (`Laplace3D::FxU`) and its gradient (`FxdU`), Biot–Savart, Helmholtz and Stokes layer potentials on a uniform (θ, φ) grid; built-in W7-X boundary (`SurfType::W7X_`); `src/example1.cpp` reads VMEC-style RBC/ZBS coefficients (https://biest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorial/surface.html).
- `ExtVacuumField` solves the exterior problem for given B·n and net current with B = −∇S0[σ] + ∇×S0[J] and the second-kind equation −σ/2 + n·∇S0[σ] = B_coil·n + n·∇×S0[J] (https://biest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algorithms/vacuum-fields.html). Tutorial (https://biest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/tutorial/ext-vacuum.html): on-surface error 2.6e-7 on a 40 × 40 grid per period, about 1e-10 at 80 × 80, for a 5-period test surface.
- Singular quadrature page (https://biest.readthedocs.io/en/latest/algorithms/singular-quadrature.html): the patch is square in grid-index space, so on anisotropic grids it is not round on the surface; the field-period operator warns when the spacing ratio exceeds 4 and enlarges the patch. Parameter table (JCP paper, Table 3, Green's-identity test on QAS3): e.g. 280 × 56 grid, patch radius 15, order 30 → 1.2e-6; 700 × 140 → 3.3e-9.
- *(my reading)*: to get PB3D's quantity (Φ on the surface for given ∂Φ/∂n), run `ExtVacuumField` with zero net current and evaluate Φ = S0[σ] with the single-layer operator; this output is not documented as such. PB3D's own lattice has a spacing ratio near 8.6, above BIEST's warning level of 4, so a polar patch in index space would need an elongated patch or resampling on that lattice.

### 2.4 virtual-casing package and SIMSOPT [read: docs]

### 2.5 DESC singular integrals (Python/JAX) [read: source and tests]
Source: https://github.com/PlasmaControl/DESC/blob/master/desc/integrals/singularities.py (MIT). Tests: `tests/test_integrals.py`, class `TestSingularities`. Paper: R. Conlin, J. Schilling, D. W. `singular_integral(eval_data, source_data, kernel, interpolator)` implements the Malhotra scheme with built-in kernels `'1_over_r'` (PB3D's G) and `'nr_over_r3'` = n'·(x−x')/|x−x'|³ (PB3D's H), plus Biot–Savart kernels; a user-supplied kernel callable can carry a density. `best_params`/`best_ratio` choose a support of st × sz grid points so that the patch is roughly round in real space, which handles grid anisotropy. `test_singular_integral_greens_id` asserts |2π + ∫ n'·(x−x')/|x−x'|³ dS'| < 6e-7 on DESC's bundled W7-X equilibrium with `LinearGrid(M=50, N=50)` per field period; the docstring quotes errors from 0.25 at 195 points to 2e-12 at 12 900 points for Malhotra's parameter table. DESC reads VMEC `wout` files.

## 3. General-purpose open-source BEM and FMM libraries (Python)

### 3.1 bempp-cl [read: docs and example source]
T. Betcke, M. W. Scroggs, "Bempp-cl: a fast Python based just-in-time compiling boundary element library", *J. Open Source Softw.* **6**, 2879 (2021), DOI [10.21105/joss.02879](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.02879). Calderón background: M. W. Scroggs, T. Betcke, E. Burman, W. Śmigaj, E. van 't Wout, "Software frameworks for integral equations in electromagnetic scattering based on Calderón identities", *Comput. Math. - Toroidal harmonics: J. Segura, A. Gil, "Evaluation of toroidal harmonics", *Comput. Phys. Commun.* **124**, 104–122 (2000), DOI [10.1016/S0010-4655(99)00428-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0010-4655(99)00428-2) (DTORH) [meta]. *(my reading)*: the exterior functions √(cosh η − cos u) P^n_{m−1/2}(cosh η) e^{imu} e^{inφ} are exact harmonic functions outside a circular torus for any n, so PB3D's 3-D path can be checked with n ≠ 0 against its axisymmetric dtorh path on a circular torus, as the GPEC Julia tests do (3-D and 2-D W_v agree to 1e-3).
- *(my reading)*: interior multipoles (derivatives of 1/|x − x_s| with x_s inside the plasma) give exact exterior Neumann data on the real W7-X boundary at any order, extending PB3D's point and ring sources.
- Symmetry: Chance 1997 [read] uses the symmetry of the vacuum matrices as a routine accuracy test; GPEC Julia symmetrises W_v and warns when the antisymmetric part is large (source read). The antisymmetric part of PB3D's discrete vacuum energy matrix is an error estimate that costs nothing.
- Calderón identity: on a closed smooth surface V W = ¼I − K² (K double layer, W hypersingular), and the Calderón projector squares to itself (background knowledge; see Scroggs et al. 2017). Needs the hypersingular operator, which PB3D does not have.

## 6. Independent checks for PB3D, in order of practicality

1. **DESC `singular_integral` (Python, `pip install desc-opt`, MIT).** Load the W7-X VMEC `wout` or build a `FourierRZToroidalSurface`; evaluate G·σ and H·φ for smooth test densities with kernels `'1_over_r'` and `'nr_over_r3'` (a custom kernel carries σ); solve the exterior second-kind equation for Φ by GMRES or dense LU (N ≈ 10⁴). Compare grid-independent numbers with PB3D: W = ∫ Φ ∂Φ/∂n dS for a few smooth ∂Φ/∂n patterns in VMEC angles, and the NtD map applied to traces of interior multipoles. DESC's own test reaches 6e-7 on the W7-X constant-potential identity. Effort: about 1–2 days.
2. **GPEC Julia `Vacuum` module (MIT, Kernel3D Apache-2.0).** Gives the 3-D vacuum energy matrix by Chance's formulation with BIEST quadrature, field periods and stellarator symmetry; input x, y, z on a regular (θ, ζ) grid. Closest quantity to PB3D's, but the Fourier basis, angle conventions (straight-field-line angle with ν) and (2π)² normalisations must be matched before matrices can be compared. Effort: about 2–4 days; the code is from 2026 and its stellarator accuracy is not yet published.
3. **BIEST C++ `ExtVacuumField`** with `SurfType::W7X_` or VMEC RBC/ZBS input: exterior Neumann solve to 1e-7–1e-10, Φ = S0[σ] via `Laplace3D::FxU`. Effort: about 1–2 days including the C++17/FFTW build.
4. **bempp-cl** on a structured triangulation of the VMEC surface: a different, low-order method (O(h²)); suitable for a 1e-3 check with 10⁴–10⁵ triangles and FMM; use the numba backend on a Mac. Effort: about 1 day.
5. **ZetaTrap3D (MATLAB)**: exterior Neumann on tori with diagonal-only corrections, the method closest to PB3D's diagonal identities; needs MATLAB.

**Gaps**
- No published 3-D Chance-style vacuum-response calculation for a stellarator with accuracy numbers; GPEC Julia (2026) reports only convergence with resolution.
- No literature on collocation or Nyström quadrature on field-line-aligned, open-line, strongly anisotropic point sets; the nearest pieces are Wu–Martinsson (anisotropic, skewed diagonal weights), Barnett's 5h rule, Gregory/Alpert end corrections and twist-and-shift closure.
- No ready-made order-δ² formula for the vacuum energy of a helically rippled cylinder or torus.
- The vacuum discretisations of CAS3D, ELITE and MARS-F were not verified from primary sources.
